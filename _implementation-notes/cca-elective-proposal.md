# PROPOSAL

## The Intentional Heart

### A one-semester, one-credit, pass/fail elective in Christian heart formation

Submitted to the leadership of Covenant Christian Academy of Warrenton,
Attn: Andrea Sponsler, Head of School. John G. Tittle, [Date]

---

## Summary

I am proposing a one-semester high-school elective: seventeen weekly class
periods introducing students to the practices and map of Christian heart
formation (connecting with self, with others, with God, and with mission),
taught by me, graded pass/fail on participation only, with one credit as the
registrar sees fit to assign it.

This proposal grows directly out of what we learned together this fall. CCA
approved the Fellowship of the Heart after-school club, the administration
supported it enthusiastically, and the consent form received legal review.
And then no families enrolled. The structured interviews I held afterward
found the barriers were practical, not spiritual: after-school child care,
and the requirement that a parent attend with each teen. An elective inside
the school day removes both barriers completely. Students are already
present; no parent attendance is required; the format is one every family
already understands.

The deeper work the club was built for still belongs in a family setting,
and I am pursuing that separately as an evening fellowship. The elective is
deliberately the wide front door: it teaches the map and the practices at a
depth appropriate to a classroom, and it ends with an invitation to families
who want more. The two are designed to fit together rather than compete.

## 1. The course at a glance

**Shape.** Seventeen weekly sessions, one class period each, for one
semester: [Spring 2027, or the next registration window the calendar
allows].

**Who.** High-school students who choose it as an elective. No parent
attendance; ordinary elective enrollment through course selection.

**What happens.** The Four Connects, walked in order, with a practice
learned each week: honest self-reflection from Scripture, listening well to
another person, hearing God in Scripture through structured practices, and a
first serious look at gifting and calling. Each student keeps a Personal
Heart Journal and builds, by semester's end, a simple daily/weekly/monthly
rhythm of their own.

**What the course believes, stated plainly.** The course rests on one
expectation, and families deserve to see it before they choose: if a student
comes willing to hear and obey Christ, and brings what blocks them into the
light, with Scripture open, a few people beside them who know their name,
and trusted adults over the room who can see how it is going, then the door
will be open for the Holy Spirit to do the clearing, as He wills, and the
way God's love works will start working in them, setting them on an
intentional path to grow up into Christ. That sentence makes no promise that
the teacher changes anyone, or that the exercises do. Nothing in this course
is a lever. Everything in it only opens a door, and the Spirit walks through
it, or does not, in His time.

**The instructor.** I taught engineering, computer science, mathematics, and
CAD at CCA, and have trained adults in facilitation and led small groups for
decades. The course design itself is being developed with doctoral-level
instructional-design rigor within my EdD program at Liberty University.
[Instructor arrangement, employment or volunteer status, as CCA prefers.]

## 2. The seventeen weeks

**Connect with Self (weeks 1-5).** The map of the journey and the Four
Connects, with the gospel seed stated plainly; the heart as the wellspring
(Proverbs 4:23) and the journal issued; the soil of the heart (Mark 4);
knowing your own story, worked privately first; and the map of what blocks a
heart (wounds, lies, old agreements), taught as a map rather than excavated
in class.

**Connect with Others (weeks 6-9).** The difference between Christian
socializing and Christian formation; being known; the skills of listening
well; asking honest questions cleanly; giving and receiving words that
build.

**Connect with God (weeks 10-13).** The claim that God still speaks, and how
this work tests it against Scripture; Heart Bible Study; a structured
hearing practice on Scripture; carrying the practice into the week.

**Connect with Mission (weeks 14-16).** Made on purpose; gifts; a first pass
at "where are you sent?"; each student drafts a personal rule of life, their
own daily, weekly, and monthly rhythm.

**Week 17.** Integration, the capstone reflection, and the invitation
onward, including to the evening family fellowship for students and parents
who want the deeper work.

**Student leadership, within the bright line.** From roughly week eleven,
willing students open class in pairs, leading the practiced, scripted
exercise segments only, prepared in a short check-in with me beforehand.
This keeps the club proposal's bright line exactly: students lead
scaffolding and teaching only; an adult always holds anything personal.

## 3. What a classroom is, and is not, for this work

The after-school club was designed as a covenant circle. A classroom is not
one, and this course does not pretend otherwise. The boundaries are
deliberate and I want them in writing:

- **No personal disclosure is ever required.** Every practice carries a
  pass, at zero grade cost, every time. Depth is invited, never demanded.
- **The deep exercises stay out.** The story-depth work, the
  confession-adjacent practices, and the extended family exercises belong to
  the evening fellowship, not to a class period.
- **The heart is never graded.** Pass/fail rests on participation only:
  attendance and engagement, the journal kept (completion checked; content
  never read without the student's invitation), and a capstone reflection
  graded on engagement with the process rather than on depth or content. No
  student's formation is ever assessed, scored, staged, or disclosed.
- **When something real surfaces anyway**, and in honest work it sometimes
  will, it goes to the receiving end CCA already has: the care and
  counseling structure, the reporting chain, and a named adult every student
  is given on day one. This is the same connection-in-writing the club
  proposal asked for, carried over.
- **Ordinary classroom supervision applies**, under CCA's standard policies,
  and I am never alone with a student.

**A note on research, up front.** My doctoral work may eventually study
formation programs like this one. If any of this course's experience is ever
used in research, it will happen only with explicit parental consent and
university IRB approval, and I will bring that to CCA separately before
anything begins. The course itself asks for none of that and runs fully
without it.

## 4. The requests

1. **Approval and scheduling.** Approval of *The Intentional Heart* as a
   one-semester, pass/fail elective for [semester]; the registrar's
   determination of credit value; a period slot and classroom; and inclusion
   in the course-selection materials.
2. **The care-structure connection, in writing.** The same acknowledgment
   the club proposal established: the named receiving end in CCA's
   counseling and care structure, the reporting chain, and records practice,
   reaffirmed for the elective.
3. **The parent information letter and consent.** A short parent letter
   describing the course's nature and boundaries, with a consent form
   adapted from the legally reviewed club form, for CCA's sign-off before
   registration opens.
4. **The instructor arrangement**, whatever standing CCA prefers for me for
   one period a week.

## 5. Why this serves CCA

A classical Christian school teaches students to love what is true, good,
and beautiful. This elective teaches them to bring their actual hearts to
the God who is all three, with the same seriousness of method the school
brings to Latin or logic: a documented curriculum, stated boundaries, real
practices, and honest assessment of what a classroom can and cannot do. The
fall taught us that families want this for their teens but need it to fit
inside the life they actually live. This is that fit.

I would welcome the chance to walk through this with you and whoever else
should be in the room, and I will bring the full week-by-week syllabus,
the parent letter, and the consent draft to that conversation.

Grace and peace,
