# PROAPT — what each step is for, and why in this order

Sessions 7–8 · leader page, draft v1, 2 October 2026 · can travel home as an attachment to the homework handout

**What it is.** A practice for *hearing God* in scripture, not a Bible study. Faith comes by hearing (Romans 10:17), and hearing is a skill that grows with practice. Six steps: **Pray · Read · Observe · Apply · Pray again · Tell.**

**The one idea to carry.** *The order protects the hearing.* Ground yourself in what the passage actually **says** before you work out what it **means**: first what it meant in the world where it was written, then what it means for you, in yours. Each step is the floor the next one stands on. Skip ahead and you will mostly hear yourself.

| Step | What you do | Why it matters | Skip it, and… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pray** | Ask the Spirit to speak, briefly. | You come expecting to *hear*, not to extract information. The Word is His. | You get information without hearing. |
| **Read** | Read it through slowly, twice if short, aloud if you can. | Let it land whole before you take it apart. | You read for what you already think. |
| **Observe: the data** | Who, what, when, where, straight off the page. | The ground. Anyone could check it, even someone who has never opened a Bible. | You import what you already believe. This is where most misreadings start. |
| **Observe: the interpretation** | What stands out, surprises, or seems strange in the data, and why it would have mattered to those who first heard it. | The passage meant something in its own world before it means something in yours. | The passage ends up saying what you wanted it to say. |
| **Apply** | What is this saying to *me*, today, in my actual situation? One specific thing. | The step that turns reading into hearing: the general word becomes a specific word. | You stay in Bible study, or apply it to somebody else. |
| **Pray again** | Tell God what you heard, what you are willing to do, and what you are struggling to be willing to do. | Hearing becomes a response, and obedience begins here. | Insight with no answer. |
| **Tell** | Say one sentence of what you heard to someone. | Spoken hearing stays. It also lets someone else hear what you heard, a gentle check. | The most often skipped step, and the one that matters most over time. |

**A small example (John 4).** *Data:* a Samaritan woman, a well, the middle of the day, and Jesus asks her for water. *Interpretation:* she is startled that he speaks to her at all, because in that day Jews did not deal with Samaritans and a teacher did not talk with a woman alone. *Apply* only comes after that: who do I quietly assume God would not speak through, or to?

## For the leader, in the room

- **The two ways it goes wrong.** The *literalist* stays in Observe and will not Apply ("that would be making it say what I want"): honor the caution, then invite. The *over-spiritualizer* runs straight to a grand Apply: walk them back to the data, then ask for one concrete thing.
- **Stuck in Observe?** "What's one word or phrase that caught your attention?"
- **Stuck in Apply?** "Where in your life right now might this passage be talking? Be specific."
- **Applying it to someone else?** "Apply it to *you* first. They are doing their own work."
- **Hesitating to Tell?** "What you heard counts, even if you're not sure it was Him."
