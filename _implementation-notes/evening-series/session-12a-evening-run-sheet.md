# Evening 11 — DOING THE WORD

*The second evening of the third movement, Mission and Rhythm, between hearing and being sent. An evening-form session with no school-series counterpart (added 5 November 2026). Until now obeying has had a step, Pray again, but no evening of its own. Tonight gives it one.*

**Aim.** The obey step. Each person goes back to what they heard since Evening 6, picks one thing not yet done, and names the smallest next step of obedience they can take in the next two weeks.

**Anchor.** Luke 6:46–49; James 1:22–25.

## What this evening asks of the room

**Small is the point.** Not "be more patient with my brother" but "on Thursday, when he takes my charger, I will ask instead of yelling." A step you can picture yourself doing is a step you can do.

**Test before doing.** What someone heard is weighed before it is acted on. Nothing that fails the How Did You Know? checks gets done (1 John 4:1).

**Parents first.** A parent says their sentence first in each pair and in the merge. Teens learn obedience by watching it.

**The read-back needs the journals.** The Return's take-home asked everyone to find and mark their Tell sentences from Evenings 6 to 9. Have spare paper for anyone who forgot; they can write what they remember hearing.

## The homework handout

The Evening 11 sheet (the Pair Card stays with each pair for the evening): the sentence written at the top; week one, do it on the day named, daily PROAPT underneath; the end-of-week-one check-in ("Did you do it yet? What's in the way?"); week two, two journal lines, what I did and what happened; and one sentence to bring back to the next evening's check-in.

## Watch-fors

**When it involves another person.** An apology, making something right, a hard conversation: the step is talked over with a parent or another adult before it is taken. That is wisdom, not delay.

**No secrets from parents.** A step can be private from the room. It is never something a teen has to hide from their parents.

**No comparing, no performing.** A small step done is worth more than a big one announced. If someone can't find one, "I'm still listening" is an honest sentence, and they can choose one during the week.

**Accusation dressed as conviction.** If a step sounds like self-punishment ("I have to stop being so stupid"), it fails the Peace check. Name it gently and help them find what the Lord is actually asking.
