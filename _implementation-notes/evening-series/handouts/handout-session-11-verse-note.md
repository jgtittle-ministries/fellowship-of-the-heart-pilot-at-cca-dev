# Choosing Your Verse for Any Doubts?

*To the parents, before Evening 8 · Tuesday, December 1*

On December 1 you go first, and your teens will be watching. Before then, please choose one verse that you say you believe but, if you are honest, do not fully trust yet: the one where a quiet "yes, but…" comes up as you read it. An easy way to find it is to read a few verses you know well and notice which one catches. It helps your teens most if the doubt is small, specific and ordinary, the kind they may carry too: that God hears, that he provides, that he is near, that he is good when things are hard. Keep it real but not raw: honest, and fine for your teens to hear, with adult matters kept for adult conversation. You do not need to have it resolved. What your teens will learn most from is watching you name a doubt plainly and bring it to God without hurrying to fix it.

## If you need somewhere to start

- **Philippians 4:6–7**  Do not be anxious about anything … and the peace of God … will guard your hearts.
- **1 Peter 5:7**  Casting all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you.
- **Matthew 6:33**  Seek first the kingdom of God … and all these things will be added to you.
- **Romans 8:28**  For those who love God all things work together for good.
- **Isaiah 41:10**  Fear not, for I am with you.

## Before the evening

Let me know which verse you have chosen, and we will talk for a few minutes beforehand. Bring a Bible with it marked; we read it from the page, not the screen. If nothing catches when you look, that is all right too: "I don't notice any right now" is a full answer, and you may pass at any time.

*"I believe; help my unbelief!" (Mark 9:24)*

With thanks, John
