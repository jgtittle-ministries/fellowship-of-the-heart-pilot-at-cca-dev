# Evening 8 — Guiding John Through Any Doubts?

*For the parents, the third walk-through. You have just watched it done twice. Now you hold the steps, and John brings the doubt.*

## Before you start

**Who does what.** One of you guides the steps; the other reads the Scripture aloud. One person can do both.

**The verse.** Ask John, "Which verse are you bringing?" It is one he says he believes. Read it from a Bible, not the screen.

## The four steps (about seven minutes)

**1.  Name.** *Reader:* read the verse slowly. *Guide:* "What do you notice? Is there a 'yes, but…'?" Then: "What is the doubt you are holding? Say it in one sentence, as specifically as you can."

**2.  Exaggerate.** *Guide:* "Say it bigger. Say it stronger." He is not agreeing with the doubt; he is bringing it out where the Spirit can meet it.

**3.  Sit.** *Guide:* "What does that feel like? Stay with it." Then wait in silence, about fifteen seconds. Don't fill the quiet.

**4.  Re-read.** *Reader:* read the verse again, slowly. *Guide:* "What do you notice this time?" Let him answer. Then: "Thank you."

## Your part, and only your part

- **Read, listen, re-read.** Don't fix the doubt, explain it, argue it, or teach about it. We are not arguing the doubt; we are bringing it.
- **Never interrupt the re-read.**
- **It may not resolve tonight.** That is fine. The naming is our part; the answer is the Spirit's work.
- **If it goes deeper than tonight can hold:** stop the exaggerating, read the verse once more, sit quietly with him, and close with "Thank you."

## Then the room talks it over (about four minutes)

*Guide, to the room:* "What did you notice about how that went?" Talk about the process, not John's doubt. What was said stays in this room.

## If you get stuck

**Lost your place?** The four words: Name · Exaggerate · Sit · Re-read. **He can't find a doubt?** "I don't notice any right now" is a full answer; read the verse once more and close. **The clock?** The container holder keeps it; you don't need to watch it.

*"I believe; help my unbelief!" (Mark 9:24)*
