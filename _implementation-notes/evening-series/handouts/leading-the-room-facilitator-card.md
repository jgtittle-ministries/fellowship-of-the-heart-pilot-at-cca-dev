# Leading the Room — The Facilitator's Card

For everyone who leads an evening. The room owns the evening; your work is to hand it over.

## The four moves

**1. Ask an open question.** One with no right answer hidden in it. "What stood out to you?" invites; "Didn't you think the second verse was the key?" steers.

**2. Wait.** Count to seven, silently. Silence is the room thinking, not you failing. "Let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak" (James 1:19).

**3. Turn it to the room.** Don't answer back; hand it on. "What do others think about what she said?" "Has anyone else seen that?"

**4. Lean back.** When the room is talking to each other, get out of the way. If every arrow runs through you, the room is talking to its leader, not to itself.

## The clock

The container holder keeps it, and an adult gives a two-minute signal. When a round runs long, cut it to one word each, or two or three voices. The practice keeps its time.

## The bright line

**Something heavy:** thank them, don't dig, and hand it to the safety adult at once. Handing off is leading well, not failing.

**Something that isn't right:** "We hear that," and on. No debate and no correction in the circle; that comes afterward, privately, from a parent or the safety adult.
