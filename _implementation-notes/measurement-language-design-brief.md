# Measurement Language — Design Brief

*FotH Evening, two weeks, pilot · Draft v1 for John's markup · 2026-09-21*

**The question this designs** (open since the Session 5 debrief, 9/15): how we
talk about intentional observation and measurement in the room — designed
before it enters the room. The team's side is already governed; the missing
piece is the words.

## What is already settled — design on top of it, not around it

- **The Measurement Covenant** (docs/shared) — ten commitments, the weep-test,
  consent-and-daylight, renewal-or-lapse. Signed team-side, annually.
- **The Three Vital Signs** (docs/shared) — the Companion team's weekly lens;
  watch, never score; readings live in the team debrief and nowhere else.
- **The room's own look** — the Leader Feedback Round every week, and the
  self-read (the three questions, next taken together after 9/29). The evening
  handbook already states the rule: what is measured is the health of the
  work — never the worthiness of the people.
- **The active walk has no paper instrument** — the Pulse left with the
  quarter break. Everything room-facing is spoken.

## The gap

Consent-and-daylight (covenant #3) requires the room to *know* it is watched
over — but no designed words exist for saying it. Today the fact lives in a
published handbook paragraph the families may never read. The design must
produce the spoken version: honest enough to satisfy daylight, warm enough
not to turn the room clinical.

## The constitution for the words (from the corpus and the scorecard cases)

1. **Who holds the instrument decides whether its truth can be received** —
   so the words must name what the *room* holds (its self-read, its feedback
   round) as primary, and what the team holds (the vital signs) as service.
2. **The reading is already bilateral** — the room reads its leaders every
   week in the same register the leaders read the room. The words should say
   so: the door swings both ways, on purpose.
3. **Their language, not ours** — in-room vocabulary is watching-over,
   noticing, tending; *measurement*, *instrument*, and *data* stay in the
   team's documents. (The Hubbard decomposition, in the room's own terms.)
4. **The room can kill an instrument** — the bad-news test, said aloud: if
   any of this ever hurts more than it helps, say so, and it stops.

## Candidate room-facing words (for John's voice pass)

- **The welcome line** (Week 1 / newcomer): "We watch over this room the way
  you watch a fire — to keep it warm and safe, never to grade the logs. No
  one here is ever scored, ranked, or reported by name. And you watch us
  right back — every week, out loud, in the feedback round."
- **The self-read frame** (already near this, in the S7 notes): "the round
  the room already knows, grown one size — about the group, never any one
  person."
- **The new-instrument line** (whenever anything new appears): "Here is what
  this is, who holds it, and what it is for. If it ever hurts more than it
  helps, tell us, and it goes."

## Decisions for John

1. **Vocabulary** — settle the in-room register (watch over / notice / tend),
   and whether "measure" is ever spoken in the room.
2. **Placement** — expand the handbook's "How the group is watched over"?
   A welcome-script beat in Session 1? The newcomer materials?
3. **The consent moment** — the covenant binds instruments to "the family's
   consent." Where does consent happen in this walk, and is it spoken,
   signed, or both?
4. **Daylight depth** — are the Three Vital Signs shown to the room on
   request (full daylight), or summarized when asked?
5. **Council** — does this brief, or its one-page result, go in the Oct 11
   packet?
